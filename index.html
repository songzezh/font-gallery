Brief guide to font styles

Explore seven font categories, what makes them distinct, and where they work best.

  1. Serif Fonts

    Serif fonts have small finishing strokes at the ends of letterforms. Familiar examples such as Times New Roman bring a traditional, established feel to a design.

    Common uses Books, newspapers, and long-form editorial content.

  2. Sans-serif Fonts

    Sans-serif fonts omit these finishing strokes, creating a clean, straightforward look. Their simple forms make them a common choice for digital interfaces.

    Common uses Websites, mobile apps, and modern branding.

  3. Script Fonts

    Script fonts draw on cursive handwriting, often featuring connected letters and flowing strokes. They can give a design an elegant, expressive character.

    Common uses Formal invitations, announcements, and event stationery.

  4. Display Fonts

    Display fonts are designed to stand out at large sizes. From bold and chunky to delicate and ornate, they prioritize personality and impact in short passages.

    Common uses Headlines, titles, posters, and short statements.

  5. Monospace Fonts

    Monospace fonts give every character the same horizontal space. This consistent width keeps text aligned and creates a distinctive, orderly rhythm.

    Common uses Programming code, technical documents, and aligned text.

  6. Handwritten Fonts

    Handwritten fonts capture the individuality of writing by hand, from casual notes to carefully drawn lettering. They add a personal, informal touch to a design.

    Common uses Personal invitations, informal messages, and expressive brand logos.

  7. Decorative Fonts

    Decorative fonts use distinctive embellishments or themed letterforms to create visual interest. Their styles range from ornate details to playful, illustrated shapes.

    Common uses Special occasions, themed graphics, and decorative accents.

Reference: Piktochart — Types of Fonts
